# Workshop: Juggling in 5 Steps

The commonly known model for learning to juggle involves 1 ball, 2 balls, 3 balls, and so on. This model is a linear learning strategy that doesn't allow for learning through experimentation.

It also causes a high level of stress and reduces an individual's motivation to endure the emotional consequences of failure for longer. The risks associated with learning to ride a bike or swim involve physical injury; here, the risk is related to the emotional sphere.

An alternative model for learning the three-ball cascade is "Juggling in 5 Steps" (`5-Step Juggling`). It was developed by Craig Quat as part of a non-linear learning strategy.

## Learning Strategy

The non-linear model supports social and emotional learning outcomes. It assumes learning through intentional narrative and storytelling, and building a learning environment that is open to problem-solving.

## Step 1: 3 Objects - Concept Before Trick

**Props:** 3 balls or any 3 everyday objects.

**Goal:** To understand and solidify the cascade pattern.

1. Sit on the floor or at a table.
2. Hold one ball in your hand, with the other two lying in front of you.
3. Your empty hand is "hungry" and reaches for the nearest ball. If your left hand is empty, it reaches for the ball on your left.
4. You now have two balls in your hands. Place the ball from your right hand diagonally into the spot that just became empty. If the empty spot is on the left, place the ball from your right hand there.
5. Repeat according to the pattern: catch straight ahead, place diagonally.

This same pattern can be repeated by substituting the floor with shoulders, elbows, armpits, knees, or feet. Through this exercise, the entire body experiences the cascade pattern and understands it more fully.

## Step 2: 2 Objects - Ordering Actions

**Props:** 1 ball and 1 hoop.

**Goal:** To develop bilateral skills, build self-confidence, and reduce anxiety through a continuous sense of success. We build a narrative around the exercises, creating a playful atmosphere where mistakes are not significant, and curiosity outweighs the fear of failure.

This step is detailed as a separate activity: [Cow jumps over the moon](Cow%20jumps%20over%20the%20moon.md).

## Step 3: 1 Object - Practicing Throwing Technique

**Props:** 1 ball.

**Goal:** To develop technical skills. If you have completed steps 1 and 2, this stage is merely a formality, as you have practiced this activity well within step 2.

You can add systematization of throws by giving them names:

1. Throw - Column: straight up.
2. Throw - Ping Pong: a flat throw to the other hand.
3. Throw - Diagonal.
4. Throws: underhand, underleg, behind the back, with a clap, with a turn, etc.

## Step 3.5: Learning Rhythm

**Props:** 1 ball. Participants form pairs; each pair has 2 balls.

**Goal:** To develop peripheral vision and learn rhythm, which is crucial for learning to juggle and maintaining a flow state.

1. Stand facing each other in pairs.
2. Starting position: both participants hold a ball in their left hand.
3. Synchronously throw the balls in a square pattern.
4. Add extra beats, e.g., when the left hand throws a ball, give a high-five to the other person.
5. We can change the type of throws and add extra tasks between throws.

## Step 4: 2 Objects - Ordering Actions

**Props:** 2 balls.

**Goal:** To develop bilateral skills.

1. Choose which color ball will start.
2. Make two alternating throws from your hands, without catching. A few repetitions with the chosen color help avoid always starting with your dominant hand.
3. Add catching the ball that is thrown second.
4. Add catching both balls.
5. Aim to create a loop: the same color ball always starts, not the same hand.

In this step, it's most important to focus on rhythm: throw the balls one after another, not both at once. It's also important to correct the reflex of passing balls from hand to hand. Thanks to the previous steps, we minimize the risk of such undesirable reflexes appearing at the beginning of learning the cascade.

## Step 5: 3 Objects - Practicing the Target Cascade Rhythm

**Props:** 3 balls.

**Goal:** To focus the individual on the cascade experience while working in pairs, with support from the second person who "removes gravity."

1. Hold three balls and throw them one by one. Do not catch any. Start once with the left hand, once with the right.
2. Try to catch the last ball, then the last two. Once caught, try not to interrupt the movement and juggle them.
3. Try to catch the first ball, then the first two. Once caught, try not to interrupt the movement and juggle them.
4. Stand facing each other in pairs, quite close.
5. One person holds their hands low and starts with three balls.
6. The supporting person holds their hands higher, in the same vertical line.
7. The person below throws the balls diagonally; the person above catches them and drops them down into the hands of the juggler.
8. Stand side-by-side in pairs like "Siamese twins"; tuck the hands in the middle behind your backs. One person uses their right hand, the other their left. Juggle the cascade together.
9. Each person individually practices 3 catches, 4 catches, and so on.
